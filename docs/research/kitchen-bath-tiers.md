# Kitchen, bath and household support tiers

Research checked 2026-09-17. Read with [the home design standard](../home-design-standard.md). These are **repository programming tiers**, not a market survey, legal classification, price estimate or finding that any existing home passes review. The tier assignments below are design recommendations; linked primary sources support the functional checks, not a claim that every expensive house has these features.

The owner's premium-home preferences are dual vanities, a separate shower and tub, an enclosed toilet room, and wide refrigeration. Reserve **42–48 inches of refrigeration width**, with a **48-inch allowance in Coastal House and Mountain House**. These are project preferences, not universal code requirements. Record any departure before finalizing a plan. A fireplace is a project decision, not an automatic requirement.

## Program matrix

Every tier retains the complete baseline. Higher tiers improve ease of use, durability, quietness and service access before adding equipment or floor area. Optional features require a household use case and a place in the plan.

| Area | Complete baseline | Mid tier | Premium default for this repository | Luxury options, only when useful |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cooking | Cooktop and oven, or a range providing both; externally ducted extraction; safe landing and preparation surfaces | Induction option; quieter extraction and good task lighting | Well-resolved appliance wall, ample preparation area; separate oven where it improves ergonomics | Second oven, steam oven or dedicated baking zone; not an automatic oversize range |
| Cold and dry food | Refrigerator **and freezer**; pantry cabinets or shelving reachable without blocking doors | Larger organized pantry, pull-out shelves where useful | 42–48-inch refrigeration allowance; current projects 48 inches; substantial pantry | Separate refrigerator/freezer columns, scullery or secondary beverage station if demand warrants |
| Cleanup | Sink, faucet, dishwasher, waste/recycling, draining space and cleaning storage | Pull-out bins and convenient dish storage | Cleanup zone works while a second person cooks; concealed bins and durable finishes | Second dishwasher/prep sink when supported by hosting pattern; additional plumbing/service space |
| Primary bath | Basin, toilet, usable shower or tub/shower, mirror, lighting, towels/toiletries and exhaust | Better storage, comfortable shower, appropriate privacy | Two basins, separate shower and tub, enclosed toilet room, linen storage; all usable simultaneously | Larger dressing/bathing suite, heated floor or towel rail, steam shower after systems review |
| Other baths | Household-appropriate bathing and guest-toilet provision; dry storage | Guest access does not require crossing a bedroom | Comfortable guest/family baths; retain at least one practical household bathing arrangement | Additional en suites only if valuable; no fixtures inserted at the expense of circulation |
| Laundry | Washer, selected drying method, hamper, detergent storage, loading and service access | Folding counter, hanging/drying rail and acoustic separation | Dedicated laundry with sorting/folding, linen storage and reachable shutoffs | Second laundry near distant bedrooms or sports/guest wing, only when justified |
| Daily storage | Each bedroom's clothes storage; coats/shoes; linens; cleaning tools; household supplies | Mud/drop zone and organized utility storage | Generous primary dressing storage, seasonal/outdoor gear provision | Separate luggage, sports or hobby stores based on actual household needs |
| Comfort and operation | Heating/cooling strategy, ventilation, hot water, electrical/service zones | Quiet equipment, sensible controls and efficient fixtures | Zoned comfort when useful; easy filter/replacement access; climate-specific humidity strategy | Enhanced acoustic separation, specialized wellness systems; added maintenance explicitly accepted |

## Measurable concept checks

The following published NKBA recommendations are screening targets, **not adopted-code or accessibility certification**. The freely available PDFs include older code references; current local requirements and selected products must be checked separately.

| Check | Starting target / evidence to record |
|---|---|
| Kitchen work aisle | 42 inches for one cook; 48 inches for multiple cooks, measured between opposing counter/appliance fronts |
| Kitchen walkway | 36 inches; do not confuse circulation with a working aisle |
| Primary prep surface | 36 × 24 inches uninterrupted beside a sink |
| Dishwasher relationship | Nearest edge within 36 inches of cleanup/prep sink; verify usable standing space with door open |
| Refrigerator landing | 15-inch landing beside it, or across an aisle no farther than 48 inches |
| Counter seating | At 36-inch counter height, allow 24-inch width and 15-inch knee depth per person; reserve 44 inches behind seating for walking past |

These targets come from [NKBA Kitchen Planning Guidelines, guidelines 6–9, 12–13 and 16](https://media.nkba.org/uploads/2022/05/Kitchen-Planning-Guidelines.pdf). Separately test open oven doors, fully extended drawers, chair pullback and routes to outside; a clear aisle with every appliance shut is insufficient.

For bathrooms, NKBA recommends at least 36 inches between basin centerlines, 20 inches from a basin centerline to a side obstruction, a 36 × 36-inch shower interior, and a 36 × 66-inch separate toilet compartment with an outward-opening or pocket door. These are starting checks, not a desirable maximum or an accessible-room specification. A premium shower should be enlarged for comfort; a bench must not consume the stated clear standing area. Provide reachable controls and a dry approach, and test the toilet-room door against neighboring fixtures. [NKBA Bath Planning Guidelines, guidelines 5–6, 9–10 and 21](https://media.nkba.org/uploads/2022/05/Bath-Planning-Guidelines.pdf).

## Equipment and service decisions that must be drawn

- **Refrigeration:** A 48-inch rectangle is an allowance, not an installation specification. Record cabinet opening, total depth including panels/handles, hinge side, required door angle, drawer removal, landing space, water/electrical zones, accessible condenser grille and replacement route. Paired columns need their own joining and ventilation detail. Clearance varies by model; corner fillers may be necessary. [Sub-Zero door and drawer clearance guidance](https://www.subzero-wolf.com/assistance/answers/sub-zero/common/sub-zero-door-and-drawer-clearance). For scale only, one current 48-inch French-door unit is listed at 48 × 84 × 24 inches; its opening/service diagrams must be consulted rather than extrapolated to all refrigerators. [CL4850UFD/S reference sheet](https://www.subzero-wolf.com/qrExport/built-in-refrigeration-qr-sheet-cl4850ufds-st.pdf).
- **Kitchen exhaust:** Model a credible duct route to outside, accessible filter/blower and exterior termination. An open sliding wall is not the exhaust strategy; recirculation does not provide outdoor removal. Coordinate makeup air and pressure effects, especially with fireplaces or combustion appliances. Do not apply a universal airflow or makeup-air threshold without the adopted code and appliance selection. [PNNL kitchen exhaust guidance](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/kitchen-exhaust-fans).
- **Hood versus downdraft:** An overhead capture hood is the repository default. A downdraft is a deliberate appliance-compatible exception that needs space for the descending unit, blower and duct below/behind the cooktop. It is not interchangeable with a hood for every range. Confirm appliance pairings, clearance to combustibles, duct size and installation height from selected manuals. [Wolf ventilation compatibility guide](https://www.subzero-wolf.com/~/media/files/united%20states/product%20downloads/sub-zero%20wolf/misc/ventilation%20guide.pdf).
- **Bath moisture:** Show exterior exhaust, replacement-air path and timer/humidity control concept; a window is not the sole plan. Toilet-room and shower enclosure arrangements must allow air movement without sacrificing privacy. [PNNL bathroom exhaust guidance](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/bathroom-exhaust-fans).
- **Laundry:** Choose vented or heat-pump drying. A ventless heat-pump dryer still needs condensate handling and filter access; verify the selected unit's room and installation conditions. Draw shutoffs, washer hoses, door swings, folding area and a place for wet clothing. [ENERGY STAR heat-pump dryer guidance](https://www.energystar.gov/products/clothes_dryers/heat-pump-dryer).
- **Hot water and mechanical room:** Separate tub filling and simultaneous showers from fixture count alone; record that capacity and delivery time remain to be sized. Reserve service and replacement space. A heat-pump water heater needs an appropriate air/temperature environment and condensate provision, not an arbitrary sealed cupboard. [ENERGY STAR design considerations](https://www.energystar.gov/partner-resources/residential_new/educational_resources/sup_program_guidance/heat_pump_water_heater_guide/design_considerations).
- **Water efficiency:** Prefer verified WaterSense toilets, bathroom faucets and showerheads. WaterSense evaluates performance as well as water use; “luxury” does not require wasteful flow. Kitchen faucets, washers and dishwashers are not WaterSense product categories. [EPA WaterSense label](https://www.epa.gov/watersense/watersense-label).

## Acceptance evidence in each home

Record each item as **modeled and checked**, **allowance with unresolved specification**, **intentionally omitted with reason**, or **missing/failing** in `design-review.md`. The first category requires an object/plan location and measured evidence where relevant. An equipment placeholder cannot prove ventilation performance, electrical capacity or compliance. Do not call the home fully reviewed while required program is missing.

For the next Coastal and Mountain review: verify all kitchen equipment above, pantry/waste, refrigerator opening, bedroom closets, coat/drop storage, laundry/linen, primary-bath suite, and mechanical provisions; then trace the daily routes with doors and appliances in use. Keep optional fireplace, second oven and specialty wellness equipment explicit instead of silently assumed.

Reusable fixture, appliance, cabinet and storage assemblies should be pinned library assets with installation-envelope metadata. Keep their clearance envelopes visible in review drawings but hidden in presentation renders. A generic assembly is a concept placeholder; its metadata must not imply a manufacturer-approved installation.
