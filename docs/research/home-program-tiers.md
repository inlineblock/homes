# Home program profiles

Research checked 2026-09-17. These are **internal design-brief profiles**, not universal industry tiers, price bands, legal definitions of a bedroom, or construction requirements. A smaller home can have exceptional spatial and material quality. Household needs, site, climate, accessibility and maintenance take precedence over prestige.

Use alongside [the home design standard](../home-design-standard.md). Select a profile in the brief, then record each feature as **included**, **deliberately omitted with a reason**, or **unresolved**. An unresolved essential is an open design failure, not a completed review. Sources inform the choices below; the matrix itself is this repository's design recommendation.

## Evidence informing the profiles

- NAHB's 2024 buyer study highlights laundry, patios, garage storage, main-level full bathrooms, pantry space and energy-efficient components. These are everyday functions, not reasons to make every house larger. [NAHB, February 2024](https://www.nahb.org/news-and-economics/press-releases/2024/02/top-design-trends-for-2024)
- AIA's 2025 custom-residential survey reports increasing interest in daylight, enhanced showers, heated floors and butler pantries. Its scores measure changes in architects' reported popularity, not the share of all homes possessing a feature. [AIA, April 2025](https://www.aia.org/about-aia/press/aias-home-design-trends-survey-focusing-kitchen-and-bathrooms-now-available)
- NKBA's public 2026 bath report summary emphasizes personalized storage, lighting, durable finishes and accessible showers. It also reports that 55% of surveyed professionals prioritize a larger shower over a tub. Therefore a separate tub is a **user preference for this repository's premium briefs**, not a universal requirement for an expensive house. [NKBA, November 2025, pages 1–2](https://kb-media.nkba.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/FINAL_2026-Bath-Trends-Report-Press-Release.pdf)
- AIA's 2025 special-function survey gives home offices and outdoor living stronger prominence than narrowly specialized hobby rooms. Flexible space should usually be considered before adding a dedicated room for every activity. [AIA Q3 2025, figure 2](https://hdts.aia.org/2025/reports/2025-q3/2025-Q3-Home-Design-Trends-Survey.pdf)

## Baseline for every profile

These are repository requirements unless the specific brief documents an alternative. They are not a claim about local code.

| Area | Must be resolved in the concept |
| --- | --- |
| Arrival and parking | Vehicle count or explicit car-free brief; car-sized bays and approach; pedestrian route usable with cars parked; sheltered, legible entrance; coat, shoe, bag and package provision; an inviting foyer rather than an accidental corridor. |
| Bedrooms | Bed and bedside use, clothes storage in **every** bedroom, reachable storage fronts and opening doors, privacy, daylight, and a locally checked egress strategy before any construction claim. A wardrobe can meet our storage intent; do not invent a universal closet law. |
| Kitchen | Refrigerator/freezer; **range, or cooktop plus oven**; hood and a plausible exhaust route; sink/faucet; dishwasher by default; preparation and appliance landing surfaces; food, cookware and dish storage; trash/recycling; task lighting; usable appliance-door and drawer clearances. Show the equipment in plan and model, not only in a list. |
| Bathrooms | Toilet, basin, bathing provision appropriate to the stated bathroom count, toiletries/towel storage, mirror/task light, exhaust concept, wet-area separation and privacy from public rooms. State actual fixtures rather than relying only on ambiguous “full bath” labels. |
| Utility and storage | Washer/dryer with service access and drying strategy, hamper/folding provision, linen, broom/vacuum, cleaning supplies, bulk/seasonal items and outdoor equipment; mechanical, hot-water and electrical equipment locations and replacement routes. |
| Living and movement | Real seating/dining capacity, privacy between public and sleeping areas, circulation with furniture in use, stair/landing/headroom logic, acoustic separation from laundry/garage/media spaces, daylight with glare/privacy control. |
| Outdoor and service | Usable outdoor landing; furniture circulation; shade/weather shelter as appropriate; bins and collection route; hose/cleaning provision; drainage, guard and support concepts; maintained access to equipment. |

Cooking creates particles even without a flame. EPA favors a hood exhausting outdoors and notes that exhaust airflow and replacement air need coordination. Reserve the route and service space at concept stage; a decorative hood is insufficient. [EPA cooking and ventilation guidance](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/sources-indoor-particulate-matter-pm)

## Profile additions

All columns retain the baseline. “Consider” means a decision to record, not a mandatory purchase or room.

| Program | Comfortable / efficient | Midrange | Premium — default for these “nice home” briefs | Luxury / custom |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kitchen | Complete compact work zones; durable, repairable equipment and organized pantry cabinets. | More preparation/storage capacity; island only if it preserves circulation; consider walk-in pantry. | Generous pantry and organized appliance storage; refined task lighting and quiet equipment; island sized to real seating/cooking needs. | Tailored entertaining/catering workflow; consider scullery, second dishwasher or refrigeration only for stated use. |
| Primary bath | Well-planned single basin and shower or tub/shower; no cramped fixture collection. | Consider double vanity; bathing choice based on household. | **Double vanity, separate shower and tub, enclosed toilet room** as requested; linen and grooming storage; usable doors and dry circulation. | Same functional test; consider dressing connection, heated floor, steam or wellness facilities with service space. |
| Other baths | Enough access for household/guests without crossing a private bedroom unnecessarily. | Consider a guest powder room and shared-bath privacy separation. | Guest powder room preferred; secondary-bath sharing planned around simultaneous use. | En suites only where household/guest use justifies their area and maintenance. |
| Storage and laundry | Distributed fitted storage and practical laundry space. | Dedicated folding surface, coat/drop zone and seasonal storage. | Well-organized primary wardrobes, linen cabinets, utility sink where useful, generous mudroom/drop storage. | Custom inventory-based dressing and laundry rooms; second laundry only when travel between levels warrants it. |
| Flex and acoustics | Space capable of work or guest use; separate noisy equipment from sleep. | Enclosable office/flex space if needed. | Quiet office, guest/family privacy, media noise and plumbing noise considered in adjacencies. | Dedicated library, studio, gym or theater only with a stated use and acoustic/service strategy. |
| Arrival and vehicles | Household-sized parking and clear front-door route. | Covered parking/garage when brief and climate support it; bike/gear storage. | Sheltered arrival, concealed bins and integrated vehicle/gear storage; consider EV charging provision. | Additional bays, workshop or vehicle lifts are optional specialist programs, never a substitute for pedestrian access. |
| Outdoor living | One usable furnished area. | Dining plus shade or rain cover as appropriate. | Connected indoor/outdoor dining/lounge; material and threshold details suited to exposure. | Outdoor kitchen, pool, spa and large entertaining areas only with safety, servicing and maintenance provision. |

Do not gain a “higher tier” by sacrificing circulation to fit extra fixtures. If the premium bath package will not fit, redesign the allocation or record an explicit brief exception. The same applies to an island, walk-in wardrobe or extra garage bay.

## Features that remain choices

**Fireplace:** decide explicitly whether it serves atmosphere, supplemental heat or neither. If selected, record fuel/type, hearth and clearances, flue/vent route, service access and furniture placement. An electric feature and a combustion appliance have different provisions. EPA identifies combustion as an indoor-particle source and advises against unvented combustion appliances. Do not place a generic fire rectangle in a wall and call the design resolved. [EPA combustion guidance](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/sources-indoor-particulate-matter-pm)

**Accessibility:** review at every budget. Step-free arrival where feasible, usable doorways, reachable controls, maneuvering room and adaptable bathing are not luxury decorations. Record the intended users and actual dimensions; do not label a concept accessible from a single corridor-width check. AARP describes visitability through step-free access, wide doors and a usable entry-level toilet, with fuller universal design extending throughout daily living. [AARP housing accessibility policy](https://policybook.aarp.org/policy-book/housing/housing-accessibility)

**Envelope and finishes:** never encode “luxury means no asphalt shingles” as a rule. Profile establishes design intent and maintenance expectations; roofing, cladding, glass, paving and exposed hardware still require a separate climate/site/product suitability decision. A costlier finish cannot substitute for compatible slope, drainage, durability or detailing.

## Evidence required in each home

The design review should point to the furnished plan and named model components for every baseline row and chosen addition. Record actual clearances with cars, cabinet doors, appliance doors and chairs in use. Explain omissions, unresolved product/engineering checks and any exception to the selected profile. Link repeated cabinets, appliances, wardrobes, vanities, sanitary fixtures, plants, paving and materials to pinned library assets; identify bespoke layout geometry separately. A feature list alone does not demonstrate that the house works.
