# Home walkthroughs

Read this only when creating, revising or auditing a requested walkthrough video. Ordinary still-gallery work does not require a video. An audit-only request identifies defects and required repairs; it does not authorize changing the model or regenerating outputs. Apply the parent skill's source preservation, asset adoption and visual-review rules; use the target home's authoring instructions for commands. Choose route, runtime, lens, duration, resolution and review sampling for the requested experience and available hardware, not a previous home's settings.

## Resolve the house before filming it

Inspect every room and feature the route will reveal against the brief, program and native model. Finish missing kitchen equipment, cabinetry, storage, furniture support and material assignments in the model before making a polished film of those spaces. Reuse pinned library assets. Image Gen or a video model must not invent the missing interior. Keep a limited tour scoped: report remaining whole-home limitations without treating unvisited rooms as newly approved.

Preserve the canonical model. Build camera animation and disclosed presentation overrides in a separate scene. Open an actual modeled door or sliding leaf when required; do not hide a wall to fake passage. Record the canonical source hash, presentation-scene hash, linked dependencies and opening states, and reopen the saved presentation scene independently.

## Verify real movement

Plan a continuous, understandable route through actual openings, with readable turns and pauses at important spaces. Check the camera's evaluated positions and the connecting motion, not just waypoints. Sweep a stated human-sized body envelope and headroom through evaluated geometry, including transformed linked furniture/appliance instances; a camera ray or empty instance bound is insufficient. Check walking-surface support, steps, thresholds and opening states separately from collision clearance. Record exclusions and unchecked conditions explicitly; a route check is not accessibility or construction approval.

Render a native motion draft, then inspect the entire sequence for crossings, unsupported travel, abrupt speed/aim changes, clipping, exposure shifts and bad parallax. Inspect important turns and doorway transitions at sufficient temporal detail. Fix the route or model before photographic finishing; a still-image pan or digital zoom does not demonstrate movement through the house.

## Condition photographic motion on current geometry

For a requested AI finish, use the reviewed native motion as geometric control with a runtime that actually supports it, and the parent's Image Gen workflow for exact native-frame photographic references. Record each reference's source model, frame index, source-image hash, selected-image hash, actual prompt and tool. Preserve the camera, openings, furniture, materials and designed site features; disclose illustrative setting details and keep them consistent between anchors.

Use references where the view meaningfully changes: starts, endpoints, turns, thresholds and newly revealed rooms or surfaces. A start-only reference may not constrain a ceiling or wall that appears later. Add endpoint or intermediate anchors when geometry/material ambiguity appears, rather than endlessly changing a general style prompt. Reuse the identical selected reference for a held identical camera pose instead of generating unnecessary variants.

Generate and review a short, representative motion-conditioned test before extending the full route. Include a difficult reveal or transition if that is the main risk. Compare movement and geometry with native frames, not just attractive individual AI frames. If the selected runtime cannot preserve a section, do not promote the defective clip or silently substitute unconditioned motion. When the user’s brief permits an edited tour, a deliberate cut between separately reviewed moving shots can preserve the walkthrough experience; disclose the cut, omitted interval and source-shot mapping, and retain the continuous native base. Do not introduce that edit when the user explicitly requires an unbroken take; report the continuous AI finish as incomplete instead.

## Resume without mixing sources

Fingerprint the actual inputs before reusing caches: canonical model, render-source presentation scene, linked assets, authoring code, route/render settings, native frames, reference images, prompts, runtime/model versions and conditioning settings. Record output hashes. If publication relocates and resaves a presentation scene, record its separate identity and verify its dependencies and geometry; different bytes alone do not prove drift. File existence, decoding and old reviews do not establish current inputs.

When a fingerprint differs, investigate the changed dependency and its actual effect before deciding what to regenerate. Invalidate affected frames/references/clips and downstream clips conditioned on their endpoints; preserve demonstrated unchanged outputs. Never rewrite a recorded hash merely to bypass a cache guard. Resume only with reconciled dependencies and recorded evidence. At continuous segment joins, condition the next clip on the preceding clip's actual selected endpoint. A separately reviewed, hash-locked shared boundary can isolate a repair only when both sections genuinely use that same image and downstream inputs remain unchanged; record whether the boundary came from native rendering, Image Gen or AI video. Never relabel an AI-derived boundary as an Image Gen study.

## Review and promote separately

Decode the entire assembled video and inspect its motion throughout, including every segment join. Look for geometry/furniture drift, altered openings, popping or dissolving supports, material flicker, unplanned camera jumps and duplicate/dropped transition frames. Review revised sections and their joins again after repair. When playback is unavailable, dense ordered-frame inspection may support review, with adjacent-frame inspection at joins and difficult motion; state sampling, coverage and limits. Sparse contact sheets, scene-cut scores and successful encoding do not establish temporal quality. Record visual review separately from full decode and playback smoke tests: reaching the end without a player error proves playability, not visual quality. Disclose checks not completed.

Track native motion, photographic references and AI video as separate current-source review states. Passing native checks does not approve AI motion; approved references do not constitute a finished film. Describe remaining limitations without claiming whole-home design approval.

Keep experiments and frame caches in ignored `outputs/work/`. Promote only reviewed current deliverables to stable `outputs/videos/` filenames, with paired references/provenance, route and verification receipts, and a clear video index linked from the home/output pages. Preserve native and AI outputs as distinct labeled deliverables; keep no output history folders. When a model change makes a published film obsolete, remove its current designation and links until refreshed, rather than leaving a stale film presented as current.
