# Timber Courtyard walkthrough

[Play the AI-finished walkthrough](timber-courtyard-ai.mp4) · [Play the continuous Blender base](timber-courtyard-native.mp4) · [Editable camera scene](../../model/timber-walkthrough.blend) · [Home](../../README.md)

The **AI-finished walkthrough** moves through the arrival, planted courtyard and living room, then cuts once to a moving kitchen approach. It uses the actual Blender camera travel with perspective and parallax, photographic references and local video conditioning. It is not a slideshow or a photo zoom. The silent H.264 film is 768 × 448 at 24 fps: 626 frames, about **26.08 seconds**. Native 16:9 frames and references are center-cropped to this video-model format.

There is one deliberate edit at **20.04 seconds**, from the living-room pause to the kitchen. The fast native turn produced visibly wavy glazing and smeared counter edges in AI, including shorter slowed/clean-control tests, so that interval is excluded. The retained shots show real camera movement. This is an **AI photographic concept film**, not footage of a built house; fine foliage, shadows, reflections and material detail remain interpretive. [Exact shot and source-frame map](ai-edit-map.json).

The separate **continuous Blender base** retains the entire 30.04-second route: approach → glazed entry → planted courtyard → living room → kitchen. Its 721 frames are 1280 × 720 at 24 fps, silent, with a fixed 25 mm lens and 5 ft 6 in eye height. It shows the revised kitchen with a modeled induction cooktop, built-in oven, extraction hood, wide integrated refrigerator, sink, dishwasher, working drawer storage and fitted pantry, plus shared living furniture.

Three sliding leaves are opened in the separate presentation scene; the canonical home model is unchanged. These are conceptual opening states. The remaining legacy bedrooms, bathrooms and whole-home program retain their separately recorded limitations.

## Verification and reproduction

[Camera route and presentation overrides](camera-route.json) · [Fresh-process native verification](native-verification.json)

The current native MP4 decodes fully. Eight high-sample keyframes, fifteen reference checkpoints and sixty-one half-second sequence samples were inspected. A conservative 700 mm wide, 1.80 m high body sweep across all 721 camera poses and 720 connecting segments clears 2,063 evaluated fixed meshes. The closest additional bounds gap is about 142 mm beside a dining-chair back. Vegetation/landscape objects and floor contact below 50 mm are excluded; this is a concept camera-route check, not accessibility or construction approval. The continuous native MP4 also passed an in-app browser playback smoke test: playback advanced through the courtyard and reached the kitchen endpoint without a media error. This is separate from sampled visual inspection, not a claim that every frame was watched at real-time speed.

The separately reopened Blender scene verifies all 721 camera poses, constant lens and eye height, three opened leaves and the current pinned relative shared-library dependencies. A [scoped resave comparison](scene-equivalence.json) matches 3,500 evaluated mesh instances and the recorded scene properties between the original render-work scene and published relative-path resave; their binary hashes are recorded separately. The canonical source hash is unchanged. These checks do not constitute a new whole-home design approval. See [native video verification](video-verification.json), [AI review](ai-review.json) and [runtime provenance](ai-runtime.json) for exact evidence and limits.

A separate [walking-surface check](route-support-review.json) found support for all 49,665 rays across 1,505 sampled positions. This is **not a level or step-free route**: approach slabs sit about 104 mm above surrounding ground, glazing tracks rise about 49–59 mm above adjacent surfaces, and small paving gaps and a raised rug remain. The fixed-height camera does not simulate a person's gait. No accessibility or construction approval is implied.

The edited AI film was decoded in full and reviewed through 54 half-second/endpoint samples, denser source-clip samples, consecutive doorway/join frames and the explicit cut. The scene scan detects exactly that one edit. [AI review evidence](ai-review.json) records actual playback observations and limits. Its [kitchen start boundary](references/kitchen-approach-ai-boundary.png) is a separately reviewed **AI-derived video frame**, not an Image Gen edit; [boundary provenance](boundary-anchors.json) lets the edited reproduction skip the rejected turn.

From the repository root, with Blender 4.5.14 LTS and FFmpeg available:

```sh
blender --factory-startup --background --python-exit-code 1 --python tools/timber02/walkthrough.py -- --build --keyframes
blender --factory-startup --background --python-exit-code 1 --python tools/timber02/walkthrough.py -- --animate
ffmpeg -framerate 24 -start_number 1 -i homes/timber-courtyard-02/outputs/work/walkthrough/frames/frame-%04d.png -frames:v 721 -c:v libx264 -crf 18 -pix_fmt yuv420p -movflags +faststart homes/timber-courtyard-02/outputs/work/walkthrough/review-native.mp4
```

Review the complete candidate before replacing the current MP4 or running `walkthrough.py -- --promote --review-note "Describe the actual completed review"`. Then run `verify_walkthrough.py` in a fresh Blender process. Keep frame sequences and experimental AI clips in ignored `outputs/work/`; overwrite current deliverables after review instead of keeping revision folders.

The AI test uses [ltx-2-mlx](https://github.com/dgrauet/ltx-2-mlx) with LTX-2.3 Union Control, edge frames from the actual Blender animation and Image Gen reference conditioning. It runs locally. Model weights and the isolated runtime are outside Git. Render a four-second test, visually compare it with the native motion, then continue the full route:

```sh
python3 tools/timber02/finish_walkthrough.py --runtime /path/to/ltx-2-mlx --models /path/to/models --ffmpeg /path/to/ffmpeg
python3 tools/timber02/finish_walkthrough.py --runtime /path/to/ltx-2-mlx --models /path/to/models --ffmpeg /path/to/ffmpeg --continue-tour --edited-tour
```

The finisher records fingerprints of actual native frames, references, prompts, runtime settings and output bytes. Changed inputs invalidate cached clips, and each following clip receives the preceding clip's actual endpoint. The native renderer separately binds its frame cache to the canonical model, presentation scene, authoring settings and linked assets. The receipt preserves the historical generating-script hash; promotion reporting was subsequently strengthened, so the current script requires a fresh build rather than silently treating the old frame cache as current. Rebuild and review after a design change; file existence alone never establishes a current or reviewed film. AI candidates remain in ignored work until their motion, geometry and every segment join have been reviewed.
