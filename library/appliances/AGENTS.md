# Shared kitchen appliances

- Search this catalog before making a stove, cooktop, oven, dishwasher or hood inside a home. Use pinned linked assets for the appliance, and keep its cabinet opening and service routing in the home.
- A cooking area must identify both cooking surface and oven. An induction rectangle is not a complete oven. Every kitchen must also identify refrigeration, sink, dishwasher, food preparation, storage and an extraction route, unless the brief explicitly excludes an item.
- Remove opaque cabinet carcasses and drawer fronts from appliance openings. Visually inspect the appliance in the finished kitchen, and check door operation and the route past open dishwasher/oven doors.
- Preserve the documented size and front direction; do not stretch an appliance to fill a gap. Handles project beyond nominal cabinetry depth. Use the measured bounds in the manifest when checking collisions.
- These are original generic visualization envelopes, not manufacturer installation drawings. Actual appliance selection, cutouts, service clearances, heat separation and ventilation requirements remain to be coordinated with the selected product and project.
- The hood collection includes a canopy, filters and chimney cover. The host home must provide a plausible continuous extraction route; a cover floating under a solid ceiling is incomplete.

## Choose and verify a complete package

- Read [the visual appliance catalog](README.md) and exact manifests before selecting equipment. Choose a range (surface plus oven) **or** a separate cooktop plus explicit oven. A speed oven or microwave is not a cooking surface; a cooktop alone is not an oven. Choose double ovens only when the program justifies them.
- Record fuel, oven arrangement, dimensions, and locations in the home's program and asset schedule. Complete the package with refrigeration, sink, dishwasher, hood/exhaust, landing/prep counters, everyday cabinet storage, pantry storage and waste/recycling. A label on a plan or an AI-generated appliance does not count as modeled equipment.
- Use exact published collection versions. Search first; when a different size, fuel, arrangement or finish is needed, preserve the source, publish a sibling/new version with truthful derivation and validation, then contribute it back and adopt it explicitly. Do not copy appliance meshes privately into each home or edit an adopted version.
- Distinguish nominal face width, measured body/handle bounds, counter cutout, cabinet opening, mounting support, operating sweep and service reserve. Read each origin rather than assuming all countertop appliances share the same datum. No scale-to-fit appliances or hoods.
- Match the hood to the chosen cooking arrangement and coordinate its route, heat separation, capture, selected-product mounting and services. A 36-inch hood is not an automatic match for a 48-inch range. Record unresolved gas, electrical, makeup-air and listed-product requirements rather than inventing performance or code approval.
- Locate usable power points for countertop appliances and pantry worktops deliberately. At glazed walls, coordinate an accessible side/filler or suitable counter solution rather than assuming a nonexistent wall outlet. Reuse the [shared bronze outlet](../fixtures/duplex-power-outlet-bronze/v001/asset.json) when its interface fits; provide a real host cavity and space for plugs and cord bends. Keep selected electrical equipment, protection, wet-area suitability and circuit design explicitly unresolved until coordinated.
- Inspect actual native closed and open/service previews and fresh-reopen receipts. A pending review is not a passed review. Closed-state checks are not door-operation checks, and isolated asset review is not host installation approval. In the host, verify oven/dishwasher/fridge opening, adjacent drawer conflicts, safe reach, landing counters, person space and a usable route.
- Before promoting kitchen renders or AI references, show the modeled cooking surface, oven, hood, storage and pantry access in human-height and plan views. Correct missing or obstructed equipment in native sources first; do not rely on image generation to invent it.
