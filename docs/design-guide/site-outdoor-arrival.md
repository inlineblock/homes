# Site, outdoor living and arrival

Use this chapter before choosing a footprint, garage position, deck or planting composition. Read the [home design standard](../home-design-standard.md) first. These are repository design decisions and research prompts, not a surveyed site plan or jurisdictional approval. Sources checked September 17, 2026. Recheck local requirements and selected product instructions when a real site is known.

**Tier rule:** essential function, safe movement, drainage and service access belong in every home. A higher budget can improve shelter, finish, convenience and spatial generosity. It cannot excuse poor siting. “Luxury” is a project brief, not a technical classification or a requirement to use every optional feature below.

For each decision, record **selected / intentionally omitted / unresolved**, the reason, actual dimensions or performance target, linked asset versions, and evidence in the home's review. “Unresolved” does not pass the completed-design gate.

## 1. Establish the site before landscaping it

**Baseline — every project.** Record whether the site is surveyed or illustrative. Establish north, street and pedestrian edges, boundaries, easements, setbacks, utilities, existing structures, tree locations, major views, neighbor sightlines, noise and prevailing weather. Locate service connections, utility shutoffs, refuse collection, delivery access and construction access before filling the entire lot with architecture.

For a real parcel, request topographic and boundary surveys, geotechnical findings, drainage information, seasonal groundwater conditions and applicable planning constraints. Mark unknowns explicitly. A procedural terrain mesh is an illustration, not evidence of stable ground. Determine whether water, sewer, septic, well, fire-water supply and emergency access are feasible before assuming a remote mountain house can occupy a chosen clearing.

**Tier options.** A compact site can prioritize one usable outdoor room and a direct entrance. A generous site may add a motor court, garden rooms or detached outbuildings, but each adds paving, utilities and maintenance. Reserve repair access rather than consuming every available setback with permanent construction.

**Site override.** Flood, erosion and shoreline conditions can change the permitted footprint, foundation and floor elevation. FEMA cautions that a neighboring house or previous building footprint does not prove that a coastal location is safe; its siting guidance also considers future repair and relocation access. Do not infer “coastal” means a slab at lawn level or that a sea view establishes a buildable site. [FEMA Coastal Construction Manual, Volume I, siting chapter](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/2020-08/fema55_voli_combined.pdf).

**Required evidence:** annotated site plan with illustrative/surveyed status, section through the road and rear yard, and a short constraints register.

## 2. Road, parking and a humane arrival

**Baseline.** Draw road → driveway → parked car → front door → coat/drop storage → living space. A parked car must not erase the pedestrian route. Model the actual intended vehicle envelope, mirrors, open doors, trunk access and route around it; test turning and backing movements, not only a car rectangle inside a bay. Check driveway grade transitions against vehicle clearance. Identify visitor parking or explicitly state that it is off-site or intentionally absent.

Provide a recognizable entrance, a landing large enough to use the door while carrying bags, weather protection where appropriate, a place for packages, and clear address visibility. Separate the pedestrian path from reversing traffic where feasible. At the indoor end, provide a real place for wet shoes, coats and groceries. A long narrow entry corridor is not a substitute for an arrival space.

**Tier options.** A basic concept can have uncovered parking plus a short sheltered entry. Mid-tier may add a carport or enclosed garage and a mudroom. High-tier can add a covered transfer zone, concealed service court, bike workshop and wider maneuvering space. A carport can be a deliberate premium architectural choice in a suitable climate; an enclosed garage is not universally superior.

**Checks.** Record bay width/length, door opening, overhead clearance, walk width, turning envelope and storage intrusion as measured project values. There is no universal “two-car garage” rectangle that guarantees comfortable use. Keep lawn equipment, cabinets and bins out of the space used to open car doors. Show garage → house access with both cars parked. For vehicle lifts, also show the operator's protected standing area, pedestrian exit, interlocks/service access, pit drainage and the selected system's vehicle limits; a schematic carriage cannot verify these.

**EV and storage.** Reserve electrical capacity and a conduit route early. Place chargers so the cable reaches intended charge ports without crossing the entry path or lying under a wheel. Outdoor charging needs outdoor-rated equipment; equipment and electrical work require their own specification. [DOE Alternative Fuels Data Center: home charging](https://afdc.energy.gov/fuels/electricity-charging-home). Provide reachable bike storage, a bin route to collection, gardening/tool storage and seasonal equipment space according to the household brief.

## 3. Step-free access and the outdoor route

**Baseline.** Decide which entrance, main living spaces and outdoor space can be reached without steps; show any remaining barriers honestly. Plan seating/rest points, stable surfaces, visible edges, door maneuvering space and sufficient lighting. Record clear widths with planters, rail posts, furnishings and doors in their normal positions.

**Optional / adaptive.** A no-step main entrance and primary-floor sleeping/bathing are valuable at every price point. Wider routes, generous landings and an adaptable garage entry often provide more lasting value than decorative paving. In a hillside home, avoid promising that both main and walkout levels are accessible merely because each has an exterior door.

**Dimensional reference.** Where ADA scoping applies, its walking-surface provisions include a maximum 1:20 running slope, 1:48 cross slope and generally 36-inch clear width, with additional conditions and exceptions. These are a reference for investigation, not automatic certification of a private house; determine the applicable local residential and accessibility requirements and the user's needs. Drainage slopes, thresholds and door clearances must be coordinated together. [U.S. Access Board: ADA Chapter 4](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/).

## 4. Grade, walkouts and water paths

**Baseline.** Draw existing and proposed grade, floor datums, retaining conditions, drainage arrows and discharge destinations. Water must have a route away from doors and foundations. A downpipe ending at the wall or a trench drain with no destination is an unresolved design. Distinguish surface runoff from below-grade foundation drainage.

PNNL recommends sloping nearby impervious surfaces away from the house and using drainage measures where site constraints prevent that arrangement. Its guide gives 2% as a typical surface slope; use the applicable site and access requirements to resolve the actual assembly. Do not blindly apply this number to every walking route. [PNNL: patios, walks and driveways](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/patio-slabs-porch-slabs-walkways-and-driveways-slope-away-house).

**Hillside branch.** Establish an upslope interception concept, stable cut/fill limits, retaining-wall locations, below-grade waterproofing and a usable walkout landing. Show the grade on both sides of every retaining wall. Keep basement windows above the modeled ground or provide an explicitly designed well. Do not put a habitable room behind buried glazing. Retaining walls, slope stability, foundations and groundwater control require site-specific design.

**Landscape drainage options.** Swales, rain gardens, permeable paving and storage can reduce or delay runoff, but are not interchangeable decorative symbols. Soil infiltration, groundwater, slope, overflow, maintenance and lawful discharge govern selection. PNNL notes that steep slopes, frozen ground and high runoff rates can reduce swale effectiveness. [PNNL: swales, drains and grading](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/swales-drains-and-site-grading-stormwater-control).

**Tier options.** Use a simple inspectable drainage system when possible. Concealed drains and flush thresholds may look refined but need more deliberate waterproofing, capacity, overflow and cleaning access—not fewer details.

## 5. Decks, balconies, patios and shade

**Select by use.** Start with dining seats, chair pullback, lounge furniture, circulation and the number of simultaneous users. A shallow viewing balcony and a dining deck are different programs. A wraparound route needs a useful destination; a broad rear deck may be preferable to a narrow strip on every side.

**Baseline if elevated.** Model a plausible support/load path, foundations or attachment concept, beams, posts, guards, stairs, landings and continuous access. Check the space under the deck for headroom and obstructions. Do not let a pretty cantilever, roof or pergola imply that structural design has been done. Guard height, opening limits, loads, fasteners and post connections require the applicable design criteria. The AWC deck guide is a useful reference within its stated scope; its 2015 IRC basis is not evidence of current local compliance. [American Wood Council DCA 6](https://awc.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/AWC-DCA62015-DeckGuide-1804.pdf).

**Waterproofing branch.** Open deck boards provide shade below, not a dry ceiling. A sheltered patio needs an intentional drained roof/deck assembly or an appropriately designed under-deck system, with cleanouts and discharge. A balcony above conditioned space is a roof assembly. Coordinate door sill, surface slope, membrane upstands, wall flashing, ledger/anchor penetrations, guard attachments and overflow; avoid hiding these interfaces behind cladding. Drainage-plane continuity matters at penetrations and transitions. [PNNL: exterior-wall flashing](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/flashing-bottom-exterior-walls).

**Tier options.** Baseline: usable paved terrace. Mid-tier: partial shade, durable furniture and a modest covered area. High-tier: outdoor rooms with coordinated retractable shading/screens and task lighting. Outdoor kitchen, fire feature, spa and pool remain opt-in; they add structure, ventilation/clearance, utilities, drainage, winterization, barriers and maintenance decisions. Never add a heavy spa to an ordinary deck as a styling prop.

## 6. Climate and exposure override the aesthetic

| Exposure | Required design decisions | Optional upgrades only after those decisions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coastal rain, salt and wind | Roof slope matched to an actual roofing system; collection/overflow/discharge; compatible corrosion-resistant materials and connections; wind-rated openings; flood/surge/siting investigation | Sheltered courtyards, operable wind screens, outdoor shower with drainage and privacy |
| Snow, freeze/thaw and mountain access | Snow storage/plowing area; roof shedding away from entries and walkways; ice-dam strategy; frost/site drainage investigation; service/emergency access | Targeted heated landing or driveway, ski/bike room, boot-drying storage |
| Wildfire exposure | Applicable local fuel-management and building requirements; ember-resistant detailing; vegetation/debris management around the building and under decks | Removable outdoor cushions, organized maintenance access, noncombustible landscape edging |
| Hot sun or insects | Orientation, seasonal shading, operable screens, ventilation and furniture comfort | Screened dining, deeper canopy, adjustable shading |

FEMA's coastal-material guidance evaluates flood, wind, corrosion and decay resistance together; no metal finish or wood species is automatically suitable because it looks coastal. [FEMA coastal building materials fact sheet via PNNL](https://basc.pnnl.gov/library/coastal-building-materials-fema-technical-fact-sheet-no-17).

Roof pitch alone does not prevent ice dams: air leakage, insulation and roof assembly influence snow melting/refreezing. [PNNL: ice-dam prevention](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/construct-roofs-and-attics-ice-dam-prevention). For forest concepts, preserve the wooded setting beyond a deliberate near-building zone; do not distribute conifers, combustible shrubs or firewood right against a wood deck for visual density. Forest Service guidance emphasizes the home ignition zone, vegetation spacing and recurring debris removal. Confirm local requirements rather than treating a generic planting radius as legal approval. [US Forest Service: protecting and landscaping homes in the wildland/urban interface](https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/22257).

## 7. Planting, privacy and maintenance

**Baseline.** Select plants for regional climate, soil, sun, water, mature size and exposure. Model their trunks at actual terrain elevation. Preserve driveway sightlines, door operation, emergency routes and access to equipment. A reusable “olive tree” mesh is a visual proxy, not a botanical specification for every coast. Decide where privacy comes from: orientation, screens, fences, retained trees or planting that will take years to mature.

Group plants by water needs and plan establishment irrigation, later maintenance and seasonal shutoff where necessary. EPA recommends matching plants to site conditions and considering soil and maintenance as part of water-smart landscaping. [EPA WaterSense landscaping](https://www.epa.gov/watersense/landscaping-tips). Avoid inaccessible planted slots and irrigation spraying walls, glazing or paving. Give hoses, pruning, gutter cleaning and material replacement a practical route.

Prefer restrained, shielded exterior lights aimed at useful tasks. Keep neighboring windows and the night sky out of the beam; provide controls instead of illuminating the entire forest all night. [National Park Service outdoor-lighting principles](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/nightskies/outdoor-lighting-principles.htm).

## 8. Library selection and contribution

Search current `library/**/asset.json` manifests before creating components. Pin the version actually linked; the following are existing options, not automatic site specifications:

| Existing asset family | Appropriate reuse | Host project must still resolve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `landscape/ornamental-grass-clump`, `sage-shrub`, `olive-tree`, `mountain-conifer` | Repeated planting instances with modest variation | Species/site suitability, mature spacing, terrain origin, wildfire and sightline constraints |
| `surfaces/honed-limestone-paver-4ft` | Repeated terrace/walk modules; use real joints and perimeter cuts | Exterior wet-slip/freeze suitability, loading, bedding, slope and drainage; its 1.2-inch visual thickness is not a driveway specification |
| `materials/warm-vertical-cedar`, `mountain-thermo-ash`, `charcoal-standing-seam` | Consistent original surface appearance | Actual cladding/roof product, dimensions, slope limits, finish and maintenance |
| `fixtures/double-pit-parking-carriage` | Schematic lift visualization | Manufacturer selection, operation zone, pit, structure, drainage and safe access |

Publish missing repeated modules before instancing them: exterior-rated light families, planter systems, drainage grates, paver variants, timber deck/guard modules, screened bin storage, bike racks, EV-charger proxies and outdoor furniture. These are **library gaps**, not claims of existing assets. Fork an adopted version for a real improvement, record parent/dependencies and dimensional interfaces, render a preview, reopen the native file and contribute the reusable result back. Keep site-specific terrain, retaining layout, driveway alignment and irregular boundary cuts bespoke and documented.

## 9. Acceptance evidence

Before marking the concept reviewed, inspect a front arrival render, rear render, side/grade view, furnished outdoor plan and road-to-walkout section. Demonstrate the parked-car pedestrian route, usable doors, deck furniture circulation, grade-contact planting, support logic and water path. Record actual measurements and unresolved engineering separately. An attractive rear image with no parking, entry, service route or roof drainage evidence fails this chapter's concept review.
