# Envelope, windows and doors

Research checked **2026-09-17**. Use this module with the [home design standard](../home-design-standard.md) and [roof and climate guide](../research/roof-climate-materials.md). It is a decision framework for concepts, not a construction specification. Product examples establish a selection question; they are not approved products for these homes.

## Start with the whole enclosure

Before choosing a glass-wall size or wood finish, record location or assumed climate, orientation, heating/cooling priorities, rain/wind/salt exposure, snow, wildfire exposure, privacy, noise and maintenance access. Unknown inputs remain unknown. “Coastal” includes very different climates; “mountain” does not establish a snow load or wildfire designation.

Every tier needs a durable, comfortable enclosure. Allocate value-tier budgets to reliable assemblies and sensible opening sizes. Mid-tier can add better daylight distribution, refined trim and selected larger openings. Premium can add slender frames, concealed pockets, broad views and coordinated finishes **when their performance and servicing are resolved**. These are repository design options, not claims about what expensive homes universally contain. More glazing is not automatically more comfortable.

For every exterior wall section, identify four continuous functions: **bulk-water drainage, air control, thermal insulation and appropriate vapor control**. One material may perform several functions, but a cedar shader performs none of them. Air barriers and vapor retarders address different moisture transport mechanisms; vapor-retarder placement depends on climate and assembly, so do not automatically add polyethylene to both faces of every wall. [DOE enclosure control layers](https://bsesc.energy.gov/energy-basics/building-enclosure-air-barriers-vs-vapor-barriers-continuous-sealed-wrb-walls)

## Decision cards: wall assemblies and interfaces

| Decision | Baseline function and selection | Options, tier fit and climate overrides | Space and interface reservations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wall thickness and insulation | Declare a plausible layered assembly before measuring room clearances. | Cavity insulation plus continuous exterior insulation is a candidate across tiers; improved performance can be prioritized over decorative upgrades. Exterior insulation reduces thermal bridging but needs coordinated cladding attachment. | Reserve framing, sheathing, control layers, insulation, drainage cavity and cladding separately. Check deep jambs, mounting blocks and fastening through insulation. [PNNL continuous insulation](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/continuous-rigid-insulation-sheathing) |
| Wood cladding | Show actual boards, joints, corners, end grain and a drainage/drying strategy. | Cedar, modified wood or another selected species can support Mountain House's wood exterior. Appearance alone establishes neither durability nor fire suitability. Consider noncombustible alternatives where exposure demands them. | Reserve ventilated/drained cavity, insect closures, trim and separation from soil, paving and roof surfaces. Select finishes and clearances from the actual siding instructions. [PNNL durable siding](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/moisture-impact-fire-and-pest-resistant-exterior-siding) |
| Water-resistive barrier and flashing | Keep water on a path to the exterior across openings and penetrations. | A drained wall is baseline durability, not a luxury option. Exposed coastal rain increases the importance of assembly selection and workmanship. | Draw head, jamb and sill transitions, bottom drainage and penetrations. Do not end the drainage plane at a decorative trim strip. [PNNL drainage plane](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/drainage-plane-behind-exterior-wall-cladding) |
| Opening installation | Integrate sill-pan, jamb and head flashing with the wall drainage plane and interior air seal. | Recessed frames, flush trim and deep reveals require details as much as conventional flanged windows. | Reserve rough-opening tolerances and sill drainage. Follow the selected system's sealant/flashing instructions; do not blindly seal intended drainage outlets. [PNNL flashed openings](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/windows-and-doors-are-fully-flashed) |
| Deck, canopy and roof-to-wall junctions | Keep attachment and drainage concepts visible. | Independent deck supports may simplify some interfaces; a ledger connection requires structural and waterproofing design. Neither approach is automatically preferable. | Show ledger/flashing or separation, siding termination, roof-wall flashing, penetrations and inspection access. A beam through the wall also interrupts thermal and air-control layers. [PNNL flashing inspection](https://basc.pnnl.gov/diy-guides/look-missing-roof-and-wall-flashing) |

For Mountain House, draw the transition from buried foundation to exposed wood and the main-floor deck attachment in section. A wood-heavy elevation can still have a discreet durable base. Reserve drainage and waterproofing at buried walls; do not continue decorative siding into earth. Record exterior fastener and connector compatibility for coastal exposure, rather than assigning “marine grade” to a generic metal finish. [FEMA coastal corrosion guidance](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/2020-07/tb8-corrosion_protection_metal_connectors_coastal_areas.pdf)

## Decision cards: glazing and everyday operation

| Element | Baseline function | When to choose the option | Geometry and service checks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fixed picture window | Daylight, view, enclosure and privacy strategy. | Economical view framing at any tier; combine with selected operable units where ventilation is desired. | Record sill/head height, furniture relationship, exterior cleaning access and glare. Fixed glass must not stand in for a required operable emergency escape opening. |
| Casement, awning, tilt/turn or slider | Choose an operating mode deliberately. | Casements and tilt/turn units offer different ventilation and cleaning behaviors; sliders avoid a projecting sash; awnings need their own reach and weather-use assessment. No type wins every climate or room. | Model the full sash/handle sweep, screen position, reachable controls and conflicts with paths, faucets and blinds. An operable sash does not establish compliance by itself. |
| Large fixed and operable glass combinations | Provide useful daylight without making the entire room depend on one huge moving assembly. | Mid-tier or premium view rooms can use economical fixed sections with a practical daily-use door. | Align mullions with structure and furniture. Check openable area, privacy and nighttime appearance from outside. |
| Entry and service doors | Weather protection, security and ordinary access with bags or deliveries. | A generous sheltered entry and durable hardware matter at every tier. Premium options include carefully proportioned glazed sidelights or a larger leaf, not necessarily an oversized pivot door. | Draw leaf, hinge/pivot, frame, latch, handle projection, landing and weather threshold. Verify keys, lock access, doorbell, lighting and package/drop zone. |
| Skylight or rooflight | Bring useful daylight where wall windows cannot. | Optional at any tier; prefer it for a dark internal zone rather than adding roof penetrations without purpose. | Reserve curb/deck mount, shaft, insulation, flashing, shading and cleaning. Coordinate roof pitch and covering with the specific unit and flashing kit; low-pitch roofs can require a different mounting solution. [VELUX mounting guidance](https://blog.veluxusa.com/homeowner/installing-skylights-on-different-types-of-roofs?hs_amp=true) |

Schedule **whole-product U-factor, solar heat gain coefficient (SHGC), visible transmittance and air leakage**, with units, source and selected configuration. Lower U-factor means less heat transfer; SHGC concerns solar heat, while visible transmittance concerns daylight. Do not substitute center-of-glass values for an entire framed product or use the same dark tint everywhere. Climate and orientation govern useful tradeoffs, and a metal frame needs a considered thermal break. [DOE window selection guidance](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/femp/purchasing-energy-efficient-residential-windows-doors-and-skylights)

For a premium cold-climate view room, compare improved glazing and frame performance before simply enlarging panes. For a sunny coastal room, compare shading and solar-gain control while retaining daylight and views. These are design inferences; energy modeling and room loads are needed to choose performance targets. Glass safety, emergency escape, fall protection, structural support, wind/impact requirements and acoustics require their own project checks.

## Opening walls and serving counters

**Choose the mechanism before arranging the room.** Pocketing multi-slide walls need real wall pockets; stacking sliders retain a panel stack; bifolds consume folded-stack and swing space. Compare clear opening, daily passage, effort, screen integration, thermal/weather performance and cleaning access. A large opening may warrant a separate convenient everyday door. Do not leave a panel invisible inside a structural column, refrigerator or cupboard.

For Coastal House's long rear wall, record panel count, panel width, track count, stack/pocket depth, exact clear opening and the operating state shown in each image. Draw supports and reserve a designed header; a thin black line is not evidence of a feasible span. Animation should check intermediate positions, projecting handles and curtains as well as fully open/closed poses.

**A visually flush threshold is an assembly decision.** NanaWall's current sill page labels its generic Flush Sill for interior applications, while its High Performance Sill can be flush at the interior floor with certain outswing units. Those statements do not certify a different slider or exposed installation. Select and document the actual system, sill and test configuration; integrate the specified water path, adjacent paving levels and drainage without covering weeps. [NanaWall sill options](https://www.nanawall.com/design/sills)

For a serving window, choose pocketing, stacking, folding or lift-slide operation. Andersen demonstrates several such configurations; their existence does not verify the dimensions or weather resistance of our custom concept. Preserve room for handles, screen, locks and accessible cleaning. [Andersen pass-through types](https://www.andersenwindows.com/windows-and-doors/windows/pass-through-windows)

Treat indoor and outdoor counters as coordinated surfaces around a **weathered opening**, not an uninterrupted waterproof slab through a wall. Document sill location, seals, drainage, thermal break, differential movement, cabinet ends and exterior material exposure. Model both counters, cabinet doors/drawers, knee space and the complete window travel. Keep the pocket out of storage and the open glass out of the stool/serving zone. Show a closed rainy-day configuration as well as the open entertaining view. Product engineering must resolve the final joint and sill detail.

## Shading, privacy, insects and hazards

| Decision | Required concept response | Options and unresolved validation |
|---|---|---|
| Sun and glare | Identify orientation and test direct sun at the kitchen, dining table, television and beds. | Overhangs, exterior fins, shades or a pergola may help; choose dimensions by sun path rather than decorative rhythm. Seasonal shading must account for winter light as well as summer heat. [DOE window-performance and shading guide](https://www1.eere.energy.gov/buildings/publications/pdfs/building_america/measure_guide_windows.pdf) |
| Privacy and darkness | Show how bedrooms and bathrooms become private at night. | Curtains, roller shades, shutters or screened sightlines fit different designs. Reserve headbox, fabric stack and motor/power access where used; keep them clear of door pockets and ventilation. A tree is not year-round blackout. |
| Insect control | Make a deliberate screen decision for every frequently open exterior aperture. | Retractable or pleated screens may suit large openings. Compatibility, dimensions and provider responsibility vary; reserve their tracks and cassette rather than adding a transparent plane to a render. [NanaWall compatible screen options](https://www.nanawall.com/glass-walls/insect-screens) |
| Wind, impact and salt | Identify exposed elevations, hardware and the needed product-selection evidence. | Impact resistance is a specific tested product/configuration attribute, not a property of all coastal glazing. Select finish, maintenance and warranty for the exposure. [NanaWall hurricane-system examples](https://www.nanawall.com/glass-walls/hurricane-zone-doors) |
| Wildfire | Assess glass, cladding, soffits, decks, vents and adjacent vegetation together. | Read the applicable current standard and local requirements. IBHS's voluntary program distinguishes levels and assemblies; a wood-look material or tempered pane alone does not establish that the entire house qualifies. [IBHS home technical standard](https://wildfireprepared.org/home-technical-standard/) |

## Required review record

For each opening, record ID, room/elevation, size, sill/head, operation, net clear opening, glazing/privacy/screen strategy, hardware, linked asset version, performance-selection status and maintenance access. For each wall type, record layer intent and unresolved interfaces.

Concept verification must show consistent plans/elevations/sections, unblocked movement, real panel storage, reachable hardware, daylight/privacy decisions and plausible water paths. Save open, closed and intermediate operating checks. List each failure until fixed.

Separately mark **unverified** product approvals, local code, structural spans/deflection, wind and impact loads, hygrothermal analysis, energy modeling, drainage capacity, installation details and field testing. A photogenic exterior, a collision-free animation and an IFC export cannot close those checks.
