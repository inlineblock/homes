## Why consider C

The utility rooms move into a shared central block behind the courtyard. This provides a 7 × 12 ft laundry, two stacked washer/dryer pairs, stain sink and storage; a 13 × 10 ft butler pantry with a real island, preparation sink and perimeter shelving; and a full 2-ft strip of bookcases facing the public dining room. Both sinks face opposite sides of the same wall. Mechanical and household storage stay separate.

The pantry island is 24 × 42 in, with approximately **52.5 in on the preparation side, 40.5 in on the shelf side, and 36.3/38.7 in at its ends**. No stools. Useful dedicated shelving is 13.5 linear feet at four levels, or approximately 54 shelf-feet, plus prep bases, island storage and separate household storage. A tempting extra shelf at the south end was omitted because it would pinch the island route.

The laundry stacks face down the room's length. Each receives the shared premium-stack asset's 434 mm open-door projection plus 600 mm operator allowance, outside the stain-sink working rectangle. These are planning envelopes, not manufacturer installation approval or an actual mesh test.

## Meaningful compromises

- **The utility block interrupts the straight courtyard-to-garden view.** This is the defining disadvantage relative to the other options. Covered indoor movement remains through both side galleries.
- The rear public spaces connect around the service block and are less continuous. The central dining room uses circulation at the table ends, not behind its occupied chairs.
- The kitchen wing includes a small informal breakfast place and has more floor area than the smaller living room. The office/guest room is L-shaped around the third bathroom.
- The roof can retain two broad gables and the private front/open rear character, but no new elevation or finished image has yet demonstrated this arrangement.
