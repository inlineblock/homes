# Coastal House design intent

This records the current pavilion revision and its design decisions. The new `create-home` workflow now requires this record before substantial modeling in future revisions; this record was consolidated during the current model review.

## Brief and constraints

Make the house more inviting and architecturally distinctive. Preserve the bright coastal setting, 30 ft retractable living wall, separate 12 ft kitchen serving window and counters, complete household program, parking and attached full-width pergola without house-side legs. Use exposed interior beams matching the pergola in a driftwood color. The current 68 x 40 ft plan, three bedrooms, two baths and 2,720 gross enclosed sq ft are concept assumptions, not surveyed dimensions. No real coastal parcel, orientation or jurisdiction is established.

## Alternatives considered

| Direction | Benefit | Weakness and decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retain one low roof and improve finishes | Simple silhouette and least disruption | Materials alone leave the long roof bland and fail to give the open living space a distinct ceiling character. Rejected as the primary response. |
| Raise one seaward-facing glazed living pavilion above lower wings | A recognizable focal volume, useful high daylight and an exposed frame visible from the terrace | Requires coordinated roof steps, real sloping ceiling, support locations and new fixture mounting. Selected; these interfaces are explicitly modeled and reviewed. |
| Several competing gables or decorative roof accents | More silhouette variation | Adds visual noise and junction complexity without a clear relationship to rooms. Not pursued. |

## Selected direction

A 3:12 gable over living/dining rises to a 20 ft ridge; front bedroom and kitchen wings retain lower 1:12 falls. The front arrival remains restrained while the seaward glazing expresses the tall room. Deep timber-lined eaves and a lower attached pergola give the rear elevation layers at different scales. Interior rafters, ridge and exposed posts use the exact same pinned driftwood material as the pergola, canopy and eaves. One support in the shower is stone-encased rather than exposed timber in a wet zone.

Large limestone facade courses replace the busy narrow strips. Mushroom mineral exterior plaster, warm limestone interiors and matte bronze-gray metal form a quiet palette around the gray-brown wood. Pale oak cabinets and shared furniture remain complementary; darker smoked-oak outdoor furniture is deliberately retained. Expensive-looking does not mean more materials or fixtures: proportion, shade, tactile surfaces and coherent connections carry the design.

The plan retains the generous entry, bedroom storage, complete kitchen, primary double vanity/separate tub/shower/enclosed WC and usable arrival/terrace routes. The kitchen remains a lower, practical work zone opening into the tall dining/living pavilion. The broad glazing and pergola stay visually and physically connected.

Verify real sloping ceiling/upper glazing, frame contact, matching timber, genuine recessed holes, pendant mount contact, roof drainage directions, pergola support/header contact, opening travel and furnished circulation. Structural spans/cores/connections, coastal roof product suitability, flashing/water capacity, corrosion, condensation, flood/impact requirements and real site approvals remain unresolved. The slatted pergola provides shade, not rain cover.
