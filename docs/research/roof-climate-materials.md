# Roof materials: tier, climate and site decisions

Research checked 2026-09-17. This is a concept-selection guide, not a roof specification, price estimate, engineering calculation or jurisdictional approval. Product examples demonstrate why a generic material name is insufficient; they are not endorsements or selections for these houses.

## Selection order

**Site and assembly suitability come before appearance tier.** Choose the roof form and drainage strategy, identify the actual product and assembly, check exposure and loads, then compare finish, serviceability and installed cost. “Luxury homes cannot use asphalt shingles” is not a defensible rule. Likewise, an expensive metal roof is not automatically suitable for a coastal site or a very low slope. IBHS's FORTIFIED program accommodates shingles, metal, tile and low-slope systems, while emphasizing tested covers, attachment, sealed decks and roof edges. [IBHS FORTIFIED Roof](https://fortifiedhome.org/roof/)

The tier column below is a **repository design preference**, not a market survey or an assertion that a material guarantees quality. Dollar amounts need local installed quotes including structure, flashings, underlayment, drainage, access and maintenance.

## Material decision matrix

| Roof family | Visual and tier fit | Performance and structural checks | Budget and maintenance decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Architectural asphalt shingles | A reasonable value or mid-tier starting option on a compatible pitched roof; designer profiles can also suit premium traditional homes. Do not reject solely because of house price. | Confirm exact shingle, slope, underlayment and wind/hail/fire ratings. GAF generally permits its shingles from **2:12**, with special low-slope underlayment requirements below **4:12**; individual instructions govern. Not a choice for the current Mountain 0.66:12 or Coastal carport 1:12 planes. [GAF low-slope bulletin](https://www.gaf.com/en-us/document-library/documents/technical-bulletins-%26-notes/r-127-shingle-application-on-low-slopes.pdf) | Use as a local cost comparator; compare full assembly and replacement access. More expensive decorative profiles do not remove low-slope limitations. |
| Standing-seam metal | Strong candidate for modern premium homes, coastal houses and mountain forms. Concealed fasteners and careful panel rhythm support the intended appearance. | Specify the actual seam system, substrate, coating, clips, underlayment and attachment. The term “standing seam” has **no single minimum slope**: MBCI Double-Lok lists **0.25:12**, while Slimline lists **3:12**. One product's rating cannot be applied to another. [Double-Lok](https://shop.mbci.com/store/mbci/en/Products/Panels/Standing-Seam-Roof-Panels/Double-Lok%E2%84%A2/p/Double-Lok), [Slimline](https://www.mbci.com/products/slimline/) | Obtain a specialist installed quote, including seam installation and coastal finish if relevant. Allow access for cleaning, sealant/flashing checks and compatible repairs; “metal” does not mean maintenance-free. |
| Clay or concrete tile | Can suit mid-tier through luxury regional architecture; the profile should fit the house rather than serve as an automatic status upgrade. | Verify product weight and structural dead load, minimum pitch, fastening, underlayment and regional wind approval. Freeze-thaw suitability is product-specific: Verea publishes testing and separate regional installation guidance. [Verea technical documents](https://vereaclaytile.com/technical/) | Include supporting structure and qualified installation, plus safe access and replacement tiles. A premium tile is still only one part of the weatherproof assembly. |
| Natural slate | A candidate for premium or heritage architecture where a visibly layered pitched roof is intentional. Usually a poor visual match for a nearly flat modern plane. | Establish slate size, thickness, headlap, exposure, fastening and resulting roof weight. CUPA's guide illustrates how weight changes with thickness and coverage; its UK pitch guidance must not be treated as a US permit rule. [CUPA design and fixing guide](https://www.cupapizarras.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/fixing_guide_cupa_pizarras.pdf) | Compare skilled installation and structural work, not material price alone. Design inspection and repair access without treating slate as a walking surface. |
| Low-slope membrane: EPDM, TPO, PVC or modified bitumen | Appropriate to consider at **any tier** when a low roof profile is intentional. A discreet membrane roof is not inherently a cheap-house choice. | Select a complete tested system and deliberate positive drainage, flashings, outlets and overflow strategy. Carlisle identifies EPDM for low-slope roofs; its drainage discussion illustrates **0.25 inch per foot** taper, not a universal permission for every membrane assembly. [Carlisle EPDM](https://www.carlislesyntec.com/Roofing-Products/Membranes/EPDM), [Carlisle drainage discussion](https://ch-cd.carlislesyntec.com/en/Resources/Media/Blog-Landing-Page/SpecTopics/2021/05/17/Energy-Codes-and-Roof-Drains) | Include taper, cover board, perimeter details and accessible drains. Record inspection and debris removal provisions, especially beneath trees. |

## Climate overlays that can override a tier preference

| Exposure | Required concept response | Evidence and unresolved specification |
|---|---|---|
| Coastal salt, spray and persistent wetting | Establish distance to saltwater, direct spray and shelter. Coordinate panel substrate/coating, clips, fasteners, flashings and runoff from dissimilar metals. Record the actual written coastal warranty, rather than a generic “marine grade” label. | MBCI's current **unpainted Galvalume/Galvalume Plus** warranty excludes areas within two linear miles of saltwater marine atmospheres and specified persistent wetting/condensation conditions. This exclusion belongs to that warranty, not every metal roof. FEMA explains coastal corrosion and compatible connector/fastener selection. [MBCI warranty](https://www.mbci.com/wp-content/uploads/Widen%20Assets/WarrantySHEL25_Fillable_MBCI-Galvalume-Galvalume-Plus-Warranty_1201229991103_RevA_MS_0425_WEB.pdf), [FEMA Technical Bulletin 8](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/2020-07/tb8-corrosion_protection_metal_connectors_coastal_areas.pdf) |
| Wind-driven rain and high winds | Show water paths, edges, valleys and penetrations. Identify where the deck, roof cover and attached carport require uplift/load-path design. A gentle pitch alone is not the weatherproofing strategy. | Site-specific design pressures and tested assemblies remain unresolved. Sealed decking, edge details and attachment matter for all roof-cover families. [IBHS FORTIFIED Roof](https://fortifiedhome.org/roof/) |
| Fog, dew and indoor moisture | Separate exterior wetting from condensation within the roof. State the air-control, insulation and drying/ventilation approach as an unresolved assembly decision. Do not claim that increasing pitch alone prevents condensation. | Roof moisture design depends on enclosure layers and climate, not simply the roof silhouette. [DOE/PNNL Moisture Control for Residential Buildings](https://basc.pnnl.gov/sites/default/files/resource/Lstiburek%20Moisture%20Control%20for%20Residential%20Buildings%20Final%2010-23-20.pdf) |
| Mountain snow and ice | Show where roof snow and meltwater go relative to people, garage doors, decks, lower roofs and equipment. Reserve a designed retention strategy where shedding would create a hazard; do not scatter decorative snow guards. | Snow retention depends on roof snow load, slope, panel profile/material and geometry. Ice-dam prevention also needs air sealing, insulation and a suitable roof assembly; pitch or metal alone does not solve it. [S-5 snow-retention design](https://blog.s-5.com/blog/how-do-you-design-a-reliable-snow-guard-system-for-your-metal-roof), [PNNL ice-dam guidance](https://basc.pnnl.gov/sites/default/files/case-studies/PNNL%20Perf%20Brief%20Ice%20Dam%2005-11-12.pdf) |
| Wildfire and heavy tree cover | Target a documented Class A **roof assembly** and reduce debris traps. Resolve vents, roof edges and adjacent vegetation as part of the house, not only the roof material. Wood-heavy architecture needs separate siding, soffit, deck and site review. | A metal skin alone does not establish Class A: the deck/underlayment assembly can be decisive. Class A-rated fiberglass asphalt shingles also exist. [PNNL metal roofs](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/metal-roofs), [PNNL fire-resistant roof construction](https://basc.pnnl.gov/information/roof-construction-fire-resistance) |
| Hot/sunny or heating-dominated climate | Evaluate roof color and reflectance together with climate and insulation, rather than assigning a black roof to every premium concept. | Cool-roof savings vary with climate, insulation and building use. A light roof can be valuable in hot climates; the result is not universal. [DOE cool-roof selection guide](https://www1.eere.energy.gov/buildings/publications/pdfs/corporate/coolroofguide.pdf) |

## Application to the active concepts

These are **research flags against the intended geometry**, not evidence that the final native models or assemblies have passed review.

- **Mountain House:** the proposed rise/run of `0.055` equals **0.66:12** (about 3.15 degrees). Keep the slim form only with a suitable low-slope roof assembly. It is below the cited GAF shingle and MBCI Slimline limits. Double-Lok's published slope demonstrates that some mechanically seamed products can accommodate such geometry; it does not select that commercial panel for this house or validate its structure. The road-side low edge requires particular attention to garage/entry snow shedding, gutters and discharge. Snow load, retained snow, drift, moisture assembly, wildfire exposure and drainage sizing are open site/professional checks.
- **Coastal House:** the intended **2:12 main roof and 1:12 carport** need their own approved product details; the same generic roof texture cannot establish suitability. Both are below the cited Slimline minimum. The 1:12 carport is also below the cited shingle minimum. Record a low-slope-capable system candidate, corrosion/warranty screening, gutter/downpipe locations and discharge away from arrival routes. Do not label the open carport hurricane-resistant without uplift and anchorage design.

## Record before calling a roof concept resolved

In the home's design review and asset-use schedule, record:

1. Roof plane slopes, high/low edges and surface areas; model and plan agreement.
2. Runoff arrows, collection points, downpipes/outlets and a discharge destination that does not flood doors, paths or foundations; overflow concept where relevant.
3. Actual product candidate and supporting minimum-slope document, or an explicit **unselected assembly** status. A visual material asset is not a product specification.
4. Substrate, coating, fastener/clip compatibility, underlayment and visible flashing/edge strategy; distinguish modeled details from reserved design work.
5. Unknown site inputs: jurisdiction, wind exposure/pressures, rain intensity, coastal salt exposure, snow/drift loads, wildfire designation, climate zone and maintenance access.
6. Open performance questions and the person or discipline needed to close them. Do not convert unknowns into a green “passed” field.
7. Exact shared visual material/component version used. If a generic roof edge, gutter, downpipe or snow-retention visualization is reusable, publish and link it; preserve product-specific limits in metadata rather than implying that the asset certifies the assembly.

The repository's concept gate can check coherent geometry, drainage intent and honest open questions. Construction readiness requires the site, product, detailing and engineering work listed above.
