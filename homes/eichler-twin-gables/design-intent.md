## Choices and critique

The first literal roof-to-ground A-frame option was rejected: it read as cabins and sacrificed useful perimeter space. The selected Eichler double-gable direction retains twin peaks with vertical walls and generous ceiling height. An all-glass front entrance was also rejected for lack of privacy; retain a solid entry with high daylight and screened street windows. The generated side elevation had arbitrary openings and an inconsistent roof; replace it with one continuous eave and a regular structural/window-head datum, adjusting infill to actual room privacy and storage.

The initial 2,400-sq-ft concept is not a constraint. The user explicitly released the area and requested two complete primary suites, walk-in closets and a separate office. The selected coordinated grid is 68 × 60 ft with two 24-ft roof wings and a 20 × 36-ft open courtyard: 3,360 sq ft gross enclosed, including walls. This provides two equally sized 20 × 15-ft bedroom planning zones, each with separate 8 × 13-ft walk-in storage and 12 × 13-ft ensuite zones, a separate office/guest room, third shower bath and an 18-ft-deep public band. These are planning extents, not guaranteed clear dimensions. Shared layout data governs final area, room positions and drawing dimensions.

## Spatial/material commitments

Both suites receive proper storage and ensuite privacy without passing through a closet to reach the WC. Each bath includes its own linen cabinet, separate shower/tub and two basins. Office stays separate from sleeping suites. Glazed courtyard galleries connect entry and rear living under cover; exterior travel through the courtyard is optional. A practical kitchen, fitted pantry, laundry and explicit service reservations support the public rooms.

Use muted warm cedar, fine dark roof/window edges, pale mineral paving and soft woodland planting. Concept steel carries the frame; nonstructural wood-look covers provide the exposed timber appearance. Beam covers/services reduce clear height and must be measured, not hidden by the camera. Flat ceilings: 10–12 ft as scheduled; tall living/kitchen vaults approximately16–18ft to be coordinated with spans.

## Interior correction direction

The first published interior review missed functional quality: both king headboards were about four feet from side walls, ensuite door swings consumed basin approach space, and closed wardrobe rows left poor opening clearances. The user also requested a properly equipped butler's pantry, premium dressing rooms, more proportionate WCs and clearer toilet-room plan graphics. These are active corrections; the earlier review does not establish the revised result.

Retain the approved exterior character and overall floorplate. Put each primary headboard near a solid side-wall backing, raise affected side-window sills while preserving common heads, and align nightstands with the pillows. Compare swing and pocket solutions for every affected opening; use genuinely clear pocket cavities and privacy hardware where a swing degrades fixture use. Tighten WC proportions only after checking the actual toilet, approach and cleaning.

Replace uniform closed wardrobe rows with coordinated open dressing systems for mixed hanging, folded items, drawers, shoes and hamper, with full-length mirrors and task lighting. Reject decorative closet islands/benches that compromise dressing. Expand the pantry westward into the support band as needed for a small useful prep/storage island, east appliance/work counter and shallow perimeter shelves. Pull the laundry's rear/north wall two feet toward the front, turning that strip into seamless floor-to-ceiling bookcases facing the kitchen/great room. Preserve laundry operation and mechanical replacement access. Active counter aisles and shelf-access aisles have different uses; measure both with relevant openings and people.

Publish missing reusable dressing modules and adopt them. Revise furnished drawings with recognizable toilets, measured WC bounds, clear pockets/swings and legible storage layouts; provide pantry/dressing views. Update affected photographic studies only after native review. Final exact dimensions and checks belong in the coordinated program and review.

The laundry is now explicitly equipped with two stacked washer/dryer pairs, four machines total, a stain-treatment sink and additional reachable storage. Pair the pantry prep sink across the common wall in a coordinated wet zone. The shared wall needs space for the proposed plumbing and access; adjacency alone is not a plumbing design. Recheck the support-band allocation with actual stack installation/door envelopes, sink users, island aisles and mechanical replacement routes before freezing dimensions. Preserve the requested public-facing bookcase strip. These requirements supersede the earlier side-by-side machine and dry-pantry arrangement.
