# Atrium 01 — program and decisions

## Brief and status

This is a premium, single-level, kitchen-led courtyard concept for two primary occupants and two flexible secondary bedrooms. It retains three bedrooms, three full baths and **2,400 sq ft gross enclosed**, including walls but excluding the court, carport, canopies and terraces. The original Eichler-inspired character and walnut / pale stone / sage kitchen palette remain intentional.

The design is recorded in meters in `tools/atrium01/design.py`. Feet/inches below describe planning dimensions; finished wall faces and the actual asset placement manifest govern usable clearances. This record distinguishes design decisions from product selection, engineering and the final review receipt in [design-review.md](design-review.md). It does not itself certify completed output review.

Site assumptions: illustrative level terrain and a south drawing approach only. Real climate, orientation, survey, soils, boundaries, utility connections, road geometry and jurisdiction remain unknown. A warm, sheltered garden setting is an illustration, not evidence of those conditions.

## Heights, enclosure and systems

One occupied floor has finished-floor datum **0.00 m**. There is no upper floor and no floor-to-floor dimension. The principal finished ceiling is **10 ft 6 in (3.2004 m)**; the lowest principal exposed beam is **9 ft 6 in (2.8956 m)**. Selected south service rooms, laundry and secondary ensuite have **9 ft (2.7432 m)** clear ceilings for service coordination. These are distinct from roof assembly thickness and weathering surface level.

Two-foot perimeter eaves, expressed posts/beams and coordinated mullions provide the intended low horizontal character. Large openings frame the courtyard and rear garden; kitchen windows have counter-compatible sills. Bedroom glazing has paired raised/lowered opaque roller shades with actual coverage checks; room light sealing and operable ventilation remain product/envelope decisions. Timber, mineral plaster and pale stone are architectural choices; they are not selected rated envelope products. Actual spans, lateral bracing, connections, roof insulation, air/water layers, glazing performance, condensation control and foundations remain professional work.

Lighting uses recessed/architectural ambient and task sources, mirror lighting and selective opal kitchen pendants. Real apertures, service depth, hardware mounting and cabinet/door operation belong in native review. Photometric levels, electrical circuits, dimming and wet-area ratings require product/system selection.

Reserve heating/cooling, fresh air, kitchen/bath extraction, domestic hot water, plumbing falls, electrical distribution and equipment replacement routes. Equipment blocks and service-zone ceilings show intent, not sized systems. Use accessible roof drainage outlets and courtyard drain/overflow reservations; establish actual roof/terrain falls, outlet capacity and lawful discharge after site information exists.

## Deliberate exclusions and tradeoffs

A garage, second floor, dedicated office, scullery, second dishwasher, beverage refrigerator, pool, sauna, gym, outdoor kitchen and elevator are excluded: none is needed to satisfy this compact courtyard brief. Bedroom 3 provides occasional quiet work space. A fireplace is deliberately omitted to preserve the low, open living room and avoid adding an unrequested flue/fuel system. Separate tubs are not added to secondary baths; the primary suite provides the household tub.

The third bath is a Bedroom 2 ensuite; Bedroom 3 and guests share the south bathroom. Utility access through the pantry is a deliberate compact-plan tradeoff that preserves an uninterrupted kitchen work wall. Product-specific service/replacement access must remain practical. The carport and covered walk add sheltered arrival without inflating enclosed area.
