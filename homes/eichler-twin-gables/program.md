# Twin Gables Courtyard — program

## Brief and evidence

Selected concept: a new Eichler-influenced courtyard home with two restrained, parallel gables on full-height walls, a private front, generous rear garden connection and a consistent side elevation. It is not a steep cabin A-frame. The user approved the front/back character, requested correction of the irregular side, taller vaults, 10–12 ft finished flat ceilings and assumed steel framing concealed with wood-look beam covers.

The current user requirement is **two primary bedrooms, each with its own ensuite, walk-in closet, bathroom linen closet and enclosed toilet room**, plus a proper separate office/guest room. Both ensuites retain the repository's premium double vanity, separate tub and separate shower. The selected layout provides two dedicated bedrooms, a furnished office/guest room with clothes storage, three full bathrooms, one occupied level and two illustrative open-forecourt parking spaces. The office can host guests; it is not described as a third dedicated bedroom. Size is flexible and follows useful rooms. No site, jurisdiction, orientation, household accessibility brief or construction budget is verified.

**Area convention:** 68 × 60 ft floor-planning grid less the 20 × 36 ft open courtyard = **3,360 sq ft gross enclosed planning area**, including wall allowances. This agrees with the four modeled floor plates; it is not an exterior cladding-face survey, net usable area or measured conditioned floor area. The 720 sq ft open court, open parking forecourt, front canopy and rear terrace are excluded. Use meters in the native model (1 ft = 0.3048 m).

**Evidence state:** this is the coordinated design/program schedule. “Required” specifies intent; it does not mean modeled or checked. The frozen scene has matching native and functional receipts; their measured scope is summarized below and in `design-review.md`. A pass of those specific checks does not turn every design requirement into verified installation evidence. Selected manufacturer specifications, engineering, approvals and field verification remain separate.

### Door and window coordination

Use 36-in room-door opening targets, with larger entry as above, and check clear opening after frames; do not infer accessibility compliance. Place hinges to avoid wardrobe fronts, fixtures and other doors. Pocket doors need real unobstructed pockets, without pipes, switches or cabinet supports. Baths and bedrooms have their own closable doors. Bathroom access never crosses the other suite.

Front glazing uses controlled clerestories, selective side views and wood-look privacy screens; there is no exposed all-glass central street-to-living sightline. The solid entry opens to the courtyard reveal. Outer side windows share head datums and align with room/structural bays; bathroom glass is screened or high and obscured. Both bedrooms need useful daylight plus a locally verified escape/opening strategy. A nominal triangular clerestory alone does not demonstrate sleeping-room egress. Rear glass can be broad because privacy is assumed toward an illustrative garden; no actual neighbor relationship is verified.
