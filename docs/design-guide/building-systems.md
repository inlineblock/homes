# Building systems: decisions that make a home usable

Read with [the home design standard](../home-design-standard.md). This guide is a concept-design decision aid for agents, not an installation manual or a claim of code compliance. Sources were checked on 2026-09-17. The choices below are repository design judgments informed by those sources; they are not universal definitions of mid-tier or luxury housing.

## Required decision record

Before freezing a floor plan, create a systems schedule in the home's text sources. Give every item a status: **selected concept**, **reserved pending selection**, **not applicable with reason**, or **unresolved failure**. Include household use, climate assumptions, equipment location, distribution/termination route, power/water/drain interfaces, maintenance space, and the person or discipline needed to resolve it. Record the exact linked asset version separately from the eventual product selection. A decorative grille, fireplace, or condenser-shaped box proves neither performance nor compliance.

All tiers need reliable heating/cooling where required, ventilation, usable plumbing, safe electrical service, and maintainability. Premium spending should buy quieter operation, control, comfort, resilience, and serviceability before extra gadgets. Do not select larger equipment simply because the home is expensive.

## Decision cards

### S01 — Heating, cooling, zoning, and equipment locations

**Baseline:** choose a ducted, ductless, or mixed concept after comparing room loads, closed bedroom doors, ceilings available for ducts, and equipment access. Reserve supply and return/transfer paths for every occupied room. A head in the living room does not demonstrate comfort in closed bedrooms. Put outdoor units where airflow and service access survive landscaping, parked cars, and screens.

**Upgrade when useful:** separate main-floor and walkout controls, quiet bedroom zones, variable-capacity equipment, and bathroom floor warming. Radiant floors do not replace ventilation or automatically provide cooling. More zones are not inherently better; minimum equipment output and tiny zone loads require coordination.

**Climate trigger:** for Mountain House, request capacity at local winter design conditions, a backup-heat decision, defrost-water disposal, snow-clear mounting, and freeze protection. Avoid roof shedding, dripping decks, and walkways beneath outdoor units. For Coastal House, evaluate salt exposure, manufacturer-approved protection and cleaning access, humidity, and noise at open terraces. PNNL calls for room/zone load calculations, matched equipment selection, and outdoor units protected from snow and falling meltwater. [PNNL heat-pump guidance](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/ductless-mini-split-heat-pumps).

**Concept evidence:** equipment/service rectangles, sections through duct routes, zone labels, condensate destination. **Engineering pending:** loads, equipment capacity, refrigerant limits, acoustic criteria, ducts, controls, and commissioning.

### S02 — Fresh air, bathroom exhaust, filtration, and humidity

**Baseline:** select a whole-house ventilation strategy separately from space conditioning; reserve outdoor intake and exhaust positions away from contaminant sources. Show bathroom exhaust, including a private toilet room, and routes out of the building. Leave filters, fans, and dampers reachable. Coordinate closed-door air paths without sacrificing bedroom privacy.

**Upgrade when useful:** balanced heat/energy recovery ventilation, better filtration compatible with the selected fan, quiet timed bathroom boosts, and humidity monitoring. An HRV exchanges heat; an ERV also transfers moisture. Neither choice by name establishes a complete humidity-control design. [PNNL whole-house ventilation](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/whole-house-ventilation-strategies-new-homes).

**Climate trigger:** humid coasts and cool walkout rooms may need dehumidification even when cooling demand is small. Reserve a drain and service space if selected. Humidification is conditional on measured/anticipated dryness and envelope condensation risk, not a standard mountain luxury feature. EPA recommends keeping humidity below 60%, ideally 30–50%; cold surfaces can require a lower winter setting. [EPA indoor-air guidance](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/what-can-i-do-improve-indoor-air-quality-my-home), [PNNL cold-weather humidity](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/managing-indoor-humidity-and-ventilation-cold-weather).

**Concept evidence:** an air-system diagram with intakes, exhausts, filters, drains, and access panels. **Engineering pending:** current local ventilation standard, measured flows, frost control, filtration pressure drop, and psychrometric analysis.

### S03 — Cooking exhaust and competing air systems

**Baseline:** show the cooktop/range **and** oven, with a hood capture area and a continuous route to an exterior termination. Reserve a realistic chase through cabinetry, ceiling, and roof/wall. Keep cooking exhaust separate from other exhaust systems. Induction reduces combustion at the cooktop but does not eliminate cooking particles and moisture. A recirculating decorative hood is not this repository's default extraction solution. [PNNL kitchen exhaust](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/kitchen-exhaust-fans).

**Upgrade when useful:** quieter remote/in-line blower configurations, larger capture geometry for the actual cooking surface, and conditioned makeup air where the design requires it. A wider premium range is a systems decision, not just a prettier asset.

**Interfaces:** hood, dryer, bath fans, fireplace, and HVAC can affect pressure together. Record a makeup-air and combustion-safety review even before flow is selected. Do not universalize the often-quoted 400-cfm trigger: applicable code edition, appliance conditions, and jurisdiction matter. [PNNL retrofit duct and makeup-air discussion](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/kitchen-exhaust-retrofit-duct-guide).

**Concept evidence:** hood instance, chase, termination, reserved makeup-air route if needed. **Engineering pending:** capture/flow, duct sizing, fire separation, interlocks, and pressure testing.

### S04 — Fireplace: optional, with a deliberate fuel choice

**Baseline:** explicitly choose none, electric, sealed/direct-vent combustion, or a listed wood-burning system. A fireplace is optional at every tier. Electric ambience may suit a low-load coastal room; a mountain hearth may be valuable, but it is not automatically reliable emergency heat.

**Upgrade when useful:** a carefully placed focal hearth with seating, heat-safe surrounding materials, and service access. Reserve clearances and an uninterrupted manufacturer-compatible flue/vent route for combustion. Record fuel supply, combustion air, termination conflicts, glass protection, ash/fuel storage if relevant, and dependence on electric controls. PNNL recommends sealed direct-vent combustion appliances and advises against unvented gas fireplaces in its cold-weather moisture guidance. [PNNL combustion and moisture](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/managing-indoor-humidity-and-ventilation-cold-weather).

**Concept evidence:** fuel decision, surround, route and service zones. **Engineering pending:** listed product, clearances, hearth requirements, structural support, local emissions restrictions, and interaction with exhaust fans. The library's closed-glass insert has **unselected fuel and product**; its glass does not certify sealed combustion.

### S05 — Hot water, premium baths, and daily-use plumbing

**Baseline:** locate water heating and distribution before scattering bathrooms. Cluster wet rooms where practical; show accessible main and fixture shutoffs, water-heater replacement access, relief/drain provisions, and dishwasher/refrigerator supplies. Choose a water-heating concept with its air, noise, and drain requirements; do not hide a heat-pump water heater in a sealed decorative closet without checking the selected product.

**Upgrade when useful:** dual vanities, separate shower and tub, enclosed toilet room, handheld shower, and a demand-controlled hot-water return for long runs. User-preferred premium bathrooms should receive these deliberately; preserve standing space, cleaning access, drawers, towel storage, and door operation. Multiple shower outlets and large tubs require an actual simultaneous-demand and recovery assessment. Efficient distribution reduces waiting and water waste; EPA identifies demand-initiated recirculation as one solution for dispersed fixtures. [EPA hot-water delivery](https://www.epa.gov/watersense/watersense-labeled-homes-hot-water).

**Climate/household trigger:** seasonal occupancy, freezing outdoor showers/hose bibs, winter shutdown, children, limited mobility, and water quality alter the choice. Select treatment against actual water information or testing, not a generic luxury-filter package. [EPA water testing](https://www.epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water/home-drinking-water-testing).

**Concept evidence:** wet-core diagram, heater and treatment reservations, service fronts, drain/vent strategy. **Engineering pending:** demand, pressure, water-heater sizing, anti-scald protection, backflow, pipe sizing, freeze protection, and treatment specification.

### S06 — Laundry, waste, and below-grade water

**Baseline:** a washer/dryer location needs open doors, sorting/folding, supplies, reachable valves, and a replacement route. Select either a vented dryer with a short, cleanable exterior duct or a condensing/heat-pump dryer with its specified drainage and room conditions. Do not draw an exhaust route and call it suitable for every dryer. [PNNL vented dryers](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/proper-clothes-dryer-venting), [PNNL condensing dryers](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/ventless-condensing-clothes-dryers).

**Upgrade when useful:** laundry sink, drying cabinet/rail with moisture management, leak detection and shutoff, secondary laundry for a large household, and accessible drain pans where applicable. Show kitchen recycling/compost/trash storage and a clear route to outdoor bins; optional disposal equipment must suit the waste system.

**Walkout trigger:** distinguish roof/surface water, foundation drainage, wastewater, and condensate. Reserve cleanouts; verify whether lower fixtures drain by gravity or need an ejector. A hillside view does not prove a legal drainage outlet. If a sump is required, show access, sealed cover, discharge away from the foundation, alarm, and backup-power decision. [PNNL basement drainage](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/drain-or-sump-pump-installed-basements-or-crawlspaces).

**Engineering pending:** sewer elevation, septic/site feasibility, stormwater permission, groundwater, pump duty, waterproofing, and discharge capacity.

### S07 — Electrical service, appliances, EVs, and resilience

**Baseline:** reserve the meter/service location, distribution panel and unobstructed working area. Inventory cooking, HVAC, water heating, laundry, garage equipment, exterior outlets, and ordinary plug loads. Locate usable outlets against furnished plans, not empty walls. Identify wet/outdoor protection and required safety-device review without pretending a concept is an electrical design.

**Upgrade when useful:** two EV charging positions, managed charging, solar-ready conduit/roof zones, battery space, and an essential-loads panel. EV charge rate follows daily driving and parking duration; an electrician must assess capacity. Keep cables clear of walking routes and lift travel. [DOE AFDC home charging](https://afdc.energy.gov/fuels/electricity-charging-home?os=os%2F).

**Resilience trigger:** decide which loads matter during outages—refrigeration, communications, medical needs, selected heat, well/sump pumps—and how long. Solar alone does not establish backup operation; reserve compatible islanding/storage equipment and installation clearances. [DOE solar resilience](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/systems/solar-and-resilience-basics).

**Concept evidence:** load inventory, panel/charging/energy-equipment reservations and routes. **Engineering pending:** load calculation, service capacity, circuit/protection design, utility interconnection, battery location/fire rules, and backup duration.

### S08 — Lighting, controls, communications, and safety

**Baseline:** layer useful general, task, and low-level circulation light. For these modern premium projects, prefer restrained recessed lighting plus undercabinet worktop lighting; reserve pendants for deliberate focal points. This is the user's design preference, not a universal quality ranking. Check glare from beds/sofas, mirror shadows, nighttime stairs, exterior approach, fixture wet-location suitability, ceiling depth, driver access, and dimmer compatibility. Recessed holes must be coordinated with the ceiling's air/fire/insulation assemblies.

**Upgrade when useful:** scenes, concealed linear light, vanity lighting, bedside controls, shades, and daylight response. Provide physical, legible controls even with automation. Put switches/thermostats where a seated person can reach them without reaching over counters; confirm the chosen accessibility standard and household needs. Do not claim accessible compliance from a nominal mounting height alone.

Reserve data entry, ventilated network equipment, wired access-point routes, doorbell/camera wiring, and security contacts where chosen. Prefer privacy-conscious camera views and local/manual operation during network failures. Automation is optional; usable controls and alarms are baseline.

Show smoke-alarm coverage inside bedrooms, outside sleeping areas, and each level including the basement; show CO-alarm coverage outside sleeping areas and each level, then verify exact local requirements and product placement. Consider notification needs for hearing loss. [USFA smoke alarms](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prepare-for-fire/smoke-alarms/), [USFA CO alarms](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/gallery/pictographs/pictograph29.html). Record a radon-resistant construction/testing decision for ground-contact rooms. A passive pipe needs a complete route and possible future fan provision, followed by testing. [EPA new-home radon](https://www.epa.gov/radon/how-address-radon-when-building-new-home).

## Completion gate

For every selected system, trace **source → equipment → distribution → room/fixture → exhaust/drain/termination**, plus **person → service front → replacement exit**. Check routes in plan and section against structure, closets, pocket doors, ceiling lights and roof drainage. Record remaining engineering decisions explicitly. A concept can be visually complete with engineering pending; it cannot be called reviewed while a necessary equipment location, service route, or user function is simply missing.
